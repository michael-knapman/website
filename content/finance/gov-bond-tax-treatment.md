# Government Bond Preferential Tax Treatment

Some governments provide preferential tax treatment for taxpayers that hold government bonds. This means that if you are not a local taxpayer, you are not receiving all of the benefits of holding the government bond. A bond with strongly preferential tax treatment often returns a lower yield, so a foreigner holding such a bond will be getting a bad deal. To avoid getting bad deals, read on.


# US Treasuries

US treasuries are exempt from California and local taxes, so tax residents artificially reduce the yield of US Treasuries.  
If this tax exemption was removed, the yield would be higher by 15-25 basis points.
This is a very small deviation from a fair yield, so foreign investors should be happy to hold US Treasuries.

# California Municipal 

California municipal bonds are exempt from US federal, California, and local taxes, so tax residents artificially reduce the yield of US Treasuries.  
If this tax exemption was removed, the yield would be higher by 150-230 basis points.
This huge deviation from fair yield is caused by top tax bracket Californians bidding up the price of California municipal bonds.
Foreign investors should not hold California municipal bonds.

# US Corporate Bond

US corporate bonds have no preferential tax treatment and therefore are fairly priced for all investors.

# Japan JGBs

Japan JGBs have no preferential tax treatment and therefore are fairly priced for all investors.  
Discount bonds have a 2-10 basis point reduction due to preferential tax treatment for Japanese insurance companies, but we can ignore this.

# UK Gilts

UK Gilts have no preferential tax treatment and therefore are fairly priced for all investors.  
Discount Gilts have a 20-50 basis point reduction due to capital gains tax exemption for UK tax residents, but we can ignore this.

# French OATs

French OATs have no preferential tax treatment and therefore are fairly priced for all investors.

# German Bunds

German Bunds have no preferential tax treatment and therefore are fairly priced for all investors.

# Canada Bonds

Canadian federal, provincial, municipal, and corporate bonds are all treated the same by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA). Canadian bonds are therefore fairly priced for both taxpayers and foreigners.

For domestic taxpayers only, the CRA charges two separate taxes, one on bond interest, and one on bond capital gains. This means that only for domestic taxpayers, International and domestic discount bonds (bonds with low interest rates that are expected to have capital gains) are advantageous. Domestic taxpayers should choose to use bond funds that contain dicount bonds, such as ZDB, ZSDB, ZCDB, or ZGRO.  
Despite this discount bond tax advantage existing, it does not reduce the overall yield of discount bonds versus aggregate bonds (Justin Bender, 2026 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7OQzFLpOCnw). This is a market anomaly and should be taken advantage of by domestic taxpayers. Because foreign investors can easily purchase both aggregate bonds and discount bonds, it is unknown how much longer this anomaly will exist before demand for discount bonds rise, yields fall, and discount bonds become a bad deal for foreigners. Then, discount bonds in Canada will become like discount UK gilts.   
Any investor can participate in various combination of the aforementioned bonds by buying Canadian bond ETFs such as ZAG, ZDB, or ZGB.

# Conclusion

To invest only in fair-yield bonds around the world, an investor should invest in ETFs like VTC and BNDX to avoid US treasury and US municipal bonds. Because the penalty for investing in US treasury bonds is so small, just invest in something simpler like BNDW.


# Postscript

P.S. Corporate bonds and Junk bonds usually have no preferential tax treatment and are a good-deal anywhere in the world.

P.P.S. Some governments provide preferential tax treatment for taxpayers that hold domestic stocks. Therefore, an investor will want to hold a global portfolio of stocks, with a slight bias towards domestic stocks. For example, despite Canada being home to 3% of the world's stocks, Canadians often hold 25% domestic and 75% international stock. This is both due to tax treatment and because Canadians don't want to feel the fear of missing out with local companies like RBC or Shopify.
