Some people own stocks such as the stock of their employer, or companies that they believe will perform well.  
Some people own treasury notes or individual bonds.  
Some people own stacks of silver and gold in their safe at home.  
Some people own cash deposited into checking and savings accounts.  
What if there was a way to hold a diverse set of these assets in any account type of your choosing, such as a taxable account, 401k, IRA, HSA, RRSP, RESP, or TFSA?
What if you could also have extremely low and transparent fees, near-perfect fair pricing, easy tax filing, and high liquidity during business hours?
This is where Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) come in.

ETFs trade on stock markets just like stocks. Some ETFs are equity funds that contain multiple stocks. When you purchase a unit of an ETF, you are buying tiny portions of multiple stocks, usually less than a share each.  
For example, if you buy a unit of VT, you buy a tiny piece of almost every stock in the world.  
ETFs containing stockss are very useful to someone who wants to have a diverse range of stocks.


Checking accounts often offer 0.00% interest on cash deposits.  
Savings accounts often offer 0.01% interest when not using a limited-time promotional rate.  
Instead of hunting for limited-time promotions by opening new savings accounts every 3 months, use ETFs. ETFs such as VBIL (USD) or CBIL (CAD) always offer the fair 3-month treasury bill interest rate, reacting instantly to changes in Federal Reserve policy. In September 2026 the VBIL interest rate is about 3.7%, and subtract a fee of 0.06% to get about 3.6%.
