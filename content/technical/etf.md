# ETF

Disclaimer: I am not a financial advisor and this is not financial advice. Mentions of specific products are not endorsements. Products are only mentioned because they are representative of the market in 2026.

Some people own stocks such as the stock of their employer, or companies that they believe will perform well.  
Some people own cash deposited into checking and savings accounts.  
Some people own term deposits or GICs.  
Some people own stacks of silver and gold in their safe at home.  
What if there was a way to hold a diverse set of these assets in any account type of your choosing, such as a taxable account, 401k, IRA, HSA, RRSP, RESP, or TFSA?
What if you could also have extremely low and transparent fees, near-perfect fair pricing, easy tax filing, and high liquidity during business hours?
This is where Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) come in.

# ETFs to replace stocks

ETFs trade on stock markets just like stocks. Some ETFs are equity funds that contain multiple stocks. When you purchase a unit of an ETF, you are buying tiny portions of multiple stocks, usually less than a share each.  
For example, if you buy a unit of VT or VEQT.TO, you buy a tiny piece of almost every stock in the world.  
ETFs containing stocks are very useful to someone who wants to have a diverse range of stocks. ETFs can help you to not put all of your eggs in one basket.

# ETFs to replace savings accounts

Checking accounts often offer 0.00% interest on cash deposits.  
Savings accounts often offer 0.01% interest when not using a limited-time promotional rate.  
Instead of hunting for limited-time promotions by opening new savings accounts every 3 months, use ETFs. ETFs such as VBIL (USD) or CBIL (CAD) always offer the fair 3-month treasury bill interest rate, reacting instantly to changes in Federal Reserve policy. In September 2026 the VBIL interest rate is about 3.7%, and subtract a fee of 0.06% to get about 3.6%.

# ETFs to replace GICs and term deposits

Term deposits and GICs often have bad interest rates unless you hunt for temporary promotional rates.  
Term deposits and GICs cannot be sold after purchasing; You must wait for maturity for the money to be unlocked. What if you despearately needed money but couldn't sell your GIC?  
ETFs solve both of these problems.  
ETFs such as VGSH and XSB.TO invests money in bonds. Bonds are re-sellable term-deposits, and their value can go up or down over time depending if mortgage rates go down or up. VGSH and XSB.TO pay out their interest monthly instead of yearly like a term deposit.  
VGSH and XSB.TO always pay out fair interest rate of their holdings, with no need to hunt for promotional rates. You can also sell the ETFs at any time, with only small gains or losses, compared to a term deposit which you can never sell.

# ETFs to replace gold bars in your safe

Holding gold coins and silver bars in your safe at home is not that safe, as the safe may get stolen or swept away during a flood, causing high insurance costs of 1% per year. Additionally, each transaction has large fees, as gold dealers will never give you the full fair value for your gold when you sell it. Debit card fees will apply too. In total, a set of purchase-then-sale transactions have a fee of around 10%, with an insurance fee of 1% per year.  
This fee drag is catastrophic and prevents physical gold in your house from being a good investment.  
Instead of dealing with all of these fees, use ETFs. ETFs such as IAUM hold their physical gold at JPMorgan Chase Bank vaults in London. ZGLD.TO holds its gold at Bank of Montreal vaults in Toronto.  
The transaction fee for a IAUM round-trip transaction is only 0.02%, this is called a bid-ask spread. For ZGLD.TO it is 0.20%.  
The yearly fee including management and insurance is only 0.09% per year. For ZGLD.TO it is 0.22% per year.

# ETFs that hold food, oil, livestock, metals in addition to gold and silver

Holding gold and silver is not a very diversified gamble. What if you could hold food, oil, cows, pigs, and uranium without putting it in your house?
This is where broad-commodity ETFs come in.  
BCI ETF tracks the Bloomberg Commodity Index, the price of a global basket of physical commodities like metals, food, oil, livestock.  
BCI does not hold physical gold food, oil, or cows, it holds futures contracts for the commodities.  
ZCOM.TO is the Canadian equivalent.  
Some people may appreciate the increased diversification from broad-commodity funds, but others are wary of the lack of physical gold.

# Some ETFs have tax filing quirks, avoid those ETFs

Some commodity ETFs such as EZPZ, a multi-cryptocurrency ETF issue non-standard K-1 tax forms and should be avoided. This makes tax filing annoying.  
Other bitcoin etfs like FBTC issue the standard 1099 tax form so don't cause any issues.
In Canada, getting a K-1 tax form means filing for US taxes which will be very annoying and require a ITIN.

# Beware of false ETFs, which come to you in sheep's clothing

There are many types of Exchange Traded Products (ETPs) on the market, and only a subset of them are true ETFs with a creation-redemption mechanism that allows for arbitrage that keeps the prices fair.  
For example, MNT.TO and PHYS.TO are both Exchange Traded Receipts (ETRs) that contain physical gold and allow for redemption of units for physical gold at the Royal Canadian Mint. However, there is no ultra-liquid creation-redemption mechanism, so the ETR price can drift far from the fair price of gold.

# Why not mutual funds?

Mutual funds are legacy products with a very long history. In the USA you could put mutual funds in your 401k since its creation in 1981, and in Canada you could with your RRSP since its creation in 1957.  
In contrast, ETFs are much newer, only introduced in 1993 in USA and 1990 in Canada.  
As legacy products, their fees are usually higher, around 1.00%-2.00% per year compared to 0.1-0.2% per year for ETFs.  
Mutual fund transactions take about 1 day for settling, and settling only occurs at night, with no instant daytime liquidty like ETFs. A delay of only 1 day is generally not a problem for liquidity.  
As such, the only advantage of mutual funds is that they prevent you from day trading or gambling.
